Know the signals. Know what to do.

What to bring to a meet

Meets can last many hours. Swimmers get wet, then may wait a long time before their next event. Tap each item as you pack it.

Timers: lane job guide

Expect a million questions from swimmers, especially the 10 and under group. You should not start conversations with swimmers, but they will start them with you. Stay calm and friendly, keep your eyes on your lane, and send problems to the coach. You are not an official. Your job is to take time, not to judge strokes or turns.

Order

Order of timing

1. Lane touchpad. 2. The pickle. 3. The timers' stopwatches.

Start

Start on the strobe

Start your watch at the first flash of the strobe, not the horn, and do not guess early. Check that your watch is running. If it is not, or you started late, raise your hand. Even in an empty lane, start your watch in case it is needed elsewhere.

Pickle

Press the pickle button

Keep the pickle in its hanging spot until the swimmer is coming in to finish. This prevents an accidental click. Press it when the swimmer touches the wall, and also stop your stopwatch. Speak clearly to the timer who writes.

Say

Say the time digit by digit

Instead of "fifty-nine thirty-six," say "five, nine, three, six."

Writer

Write the time

Record your own time first. Then get the second timer's time to write down. Write times to the hundredths, with no rounding. Never copy a time from the scoreboard. Keep track that the right swimmer is swimming in each heat. If there is a no show, write N/S on the paper so there is no mix up.

Don't

Crowd the swimmers

Stay behind the blocks and away from the swimmers when they start. That gives officials a clear view of the swimmers.

Do

Watch the wall

When swimmers are about to finish, make sure you have a clear view of them touching the wall. Stop your watch when any part of the swimmer touches the wall. Know the race distance so you do not stop too early or too late.

Do

Send problems to the coach

If a swimmer has an issue, like missing their heat, send them to their coach.

Back

Backstroke ledge

On backstroke, timers pull in the backstroke ledge. It takes two timers, one on the left and one on the right, so the straps do not tangle.

Stop

Stopwatch problem

If something happens and your stopwatch has a problem, raise your hand right away and keep it raised. The head timer, an experienced timer who keeps extra backup stopwatches running, will bring you a new one that is already running.

Do

Clear your watch

After both times are written, clear your stopwatch so it is ready for the next heat.

Help

Swimmer in trouble

Do not pull a swimmer out of the water unless they reach for your hand. If a swimmer seems to be struggling, tell the referee or chief judge right away.

Safe

Phones, cameras and one-on-one

No phones or cameras while you are timing. Never be alone with a swimmer in a locker room or restroom.

Do

Be ready to get wet

Come prepared for it.

Based on PSSC timer practice, USA Swimming timer instructions and USA Swimming Safe Sport meet guidance. Always follow the meet referee and the officials.

Parents: meet day do's and don'ts

This is how most meets run. Each pool and meet host can have its own rules, so follow the posted signs, the meet information and the officials.

Do

Be loud and positive!

Cheer for every PSSC swimmer, fast or slow, first or last. Clap for effort, personal bests and teammates. We are one team, swimmers, coaches and parents.

Do

Be on time

Get your swimmer there on time for warm-up. Check the meet information for warm-up and start times.

Don't

Go on the pool deck

At most meets, parents and other spectators are not allowed on deck. Deck is for athletes, coaches, officials and credentialed volunteers.

Don't

Coach your swimmer

Let the coaches coach. Cheer and encourage, and leave stroke and race advice to the team coaches.

Don't

Go in the swimmers' locker room

Stay out of the swimmers' locker room and changing areas.

Don't

Take photos or video behind the blocks

Phones and cameras are not allowed behind the starting blocks. Many meets also ban recording in locker rooms, changing areas and restrooms.

Do

Use the non-athlete restrooms

Only use the restrooms designated for non-athletes.

Do

Watch from the spectator area

Use the balcony or seating area the host sets aside for spectators.

Do

Send questions through the coaches

If something comes up, ask your swimmer's coach.

Based on USA Swimming Safe Sport meet guidance and Pacific Northwest Swimming meet information. Always follow the host's posted rules.
